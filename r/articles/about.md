## Citing pilotr

If you use pilotr in published work, please cite it.

> Bernabeu, P. (2026). *pilotr: Simulate experimental and behavioural
> data from a portable design specification* (R package version 0.3.1).
> Comprehensive R Archive Network (CRAN).
> <https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.pilotr>

## The developer

pilotr is developed by [Pablo
Bernabeu](https://pablobernabeu.github.io), a researcher in the
Department of Education at the University of Oxford. His work spans
cognitive psychology, neuroscience, linguistics, education and research
methods, with hands-on experience of behavioural experiments, EEG,
corpus analysis, computational modelling and statistics. He develops
open, reproducible research software in R and Python, and is a Fellow of
the Software Sustainability Institute. His [ORCID
record](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-1083-2460) lists his other work.
pilotr and its [Python
twin](https://pablobernabeu.github.io/pilotr/python/) are part of that
work, keeping a design specification reproducible and its simulated data
identical across both languages.

## Licence

pilotr is released under the [MIT

specification alike.

## Versioning and archival

CRAN assigned the package the DOI
<https://doi.org/10.32614/CRAN.package.pilotr> on publication. It names
the R package across all its versions, so the version in the citation
identifies the release used. Releases of the repository, which carry the
Python package as well, are tagged on GitHub and archived on Zenodo,
where the concept DOI <https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21266313> resolves
to the latest archived release and each release has a DOI of its own.
The [changelog](https://pablobernabeu.github.io/pilotr/r/news/index.md)
records what changed in each release.

## Contributing and support

Bugs and feature requests are best raised on the [GitHub issues
page](https://github.com/pablobernabeu/pilotr/issues). The [contributing
guide](https://pablobernabeu.github.io/pilotr/r/CONTRIBUTING.md)
describes the development setup and the conventions the repository
follows, including the cross-language random-number contract that any
change to the generative core must keep intact.
